Housing

Finding housing and rental contracts in Istanbul

A practical guide for international students renting a room, a shared flat, or a private apartment in Istanbul: where to look, how to avoid scams, what to ask before paying, and how to protect yourself with a written contract and payment records.

Last verified: 16 October 2026Legal requirementRecommendationNeeds confirmation labels explained below

Get a written rental contract when you pay

Whenever you pay to rent a room or property, request a written rental contract from the landlord or authorized representative immediately. Do not simply pay and rely on a verbal promise that the contract will be provided later. Read the contract and make sure the details match the property and the agreed terms before signing.

  • Get a signed copy of the rental contract (kira sözleşmesi) as soon as the tenancy is agreed, ideally before or at the time of payment or moving in.
  • Ask for a written receipt or traceable payment record for rent, deposit, and every other payment.
  • Check who you are dealing with: verify the landlord's identity, or confirm that the representative has authority to rent the property.
  • Have the exact address and room or property, the rent, the payment date, the contract duration, the deposit, who pays which utility bills, the notice terms, and any additional fees in writing.
  • A bank transfer or payment screenshot does not replace a rental contract. It shows you paid, not what you agreed.
  • Be cautious if someone demands money before letting you view the property, or refuses to give you anything in writing.
  • Photograph and document the property's condition and meter readings when you move in.
  • Keep copies of the contract, payment records, messages, and handover evidence for the whole tenancy and after you leave.

Why this matters: Turkish law does not generally require a contract to be in writing to be valid (Turkish Code of Obligations, Art. 12), so a verbal agreement can still bind both sides. But without a written contract it is very hard to prove what was agreed, and a notarised rental contract is one of the accepted proofs of address for a student residence permit.

How to read this guide. Legal requirement marks rules from Turkish law or an official authority, with the source. Recommendation marks practical advice from this guide, not a legal duty. Needs confirmation marks details you should check for your own situation.

This guide is for information only and is not legal advice. Rental arrangements differ: a dormitory, a room sublet from another tenant, and a flat rented through an agent can follow different terms, and some short-term lets fall outside the usual tenant protections. For a dispute or an unusual arrangement, get qualified local advice.

Where to search for accommodation

  • Your university. Recommendation Start with your university's international student office or housing office. Many can point you to university dormitories or recommended options.
  • Dormitories (yurt). University and private dormitories are often the simplest first option: rules and costs are fixed in advance, and a stamped or e-signed dormitory letter is accepted as proof of address for the residence permit.
  • Property listing websites and apps. Large Turkish platforms such as sahibinden, hepsiemlak, and emlakjet list rooms and flats (search for kiralık, "for rent"). We are not affiliated with any platform, and fake or misleading listings can appear on any of them.
  • Real-estate agents (emlakçı). Legal requirement Only businesses with a real-estate trade authorisation certificate may act as agents, and their adverts must show the certificate number (Real Estate Trade Regulation). Check the certificate on the Ministry of Trade's official search.
  • Student groups and word of mouth. Recommendation Useful for finding flatmates, but apply the same checks: see the property, check the person, get it in writing.

Recommendation Think about the commute. Check the route at the time you expect to travel, including walking to and from stations. A cheap room can become expensive in time and transport if it is far from campus. See the student İstanbulkart guide for fares.

Evaluate a listing and avoid rental scams

Recommendation Before you send any money:

  • Visit the property in person, or arrange a live video viewing with someone you trust.
  • Compare the address, photos, and description with the real property.
  • Verify that the person offering the property is the owner, or has written authority from the owner.
  • Check an agent's authorisation certificate on the Ministry of Trade search.
  • Search the photos or phone number online to see whether the same listing appears elsewhere under a different name or price.

Warning signs

  • A price far below similar listings in the same area.
  • A request for a "reservation fee" or deposit before you can view the property.
  • The "landlord" is abroad or unavailable and wants payment first, sometimes promising to send the keys by courier.
  • Pressure to decide immediately because "many students are interested".
  • A refusal to give a written contract, a receipt, or proof of ownership or authority.
  • A request to pay in cash only, to a third person, or to a different account from the one in the contract.
  • An agent who asks a fee just for showing you a property. Legal requirement Agents cannot charge for viewings (Real Estate Trade Regulation, Art. 19).

Legal requirement If you use an agent, the agent must give you enough time to read the documents, explain their terms, and give you a copy of each signed document (Real Estate Trade Regulation, Art. 14).

Deposits, rent, bills, agent fees, and payment records

  • Legal requirement Deposit: for residential leases, a deposit cannot be more than three months' rent (Code of Obligations, Art. 342).
  • Legal requirement Rent increases: when a residential lease is renewed, an agreed increase is valid only up to the 12-month average change in the consumer price index (TÜFE) (Art. 344).
  • Legal requirement Payment records: rent payments for homes must be documented through a bank or PTT, whatever the amount (Revenue Administration guidance, 2024).
  • Legal requirement Agent fees: for a rental, the agent's fee cannot exceed one month's rent excluding VAT, and unless the brokerage contract says otherwise it is shared equally between landlord and tenant (Real Estate Trade Regulation, Art. 20).
  • Legal requirement Bills: unless agreed otherwise, the tenant pays usage costs such as heating, electricity, and water (Art. 341). Utility providers usually ask for a deposit when you open a subscription; see utilities.
  • Recommendation Pay by bank transfer to the account named in the contract, with a clear description such as "October 2026 rent – [address]". Keep every bank receipt (dekont).
  • Recommendation If you must pay in cash, get a signed receipt showing the date, amount, what it is for (rent, deposit, or fee), the address, and the recipient's name.

Needs confirmation We do not list typical rents, deposits, or building fees: they vary widely by district and change often. Compare several listings in the same area.

Moving-in inspection and inventory

  • Recommendation Walk through every room with the landlord or representative on move-in day. Take dated photos and videos of walls, floors, windows, furniture, appliances, and any damage.
  • Recommendation Write an inventory (demirbaş listesi) of furniture and appliances and their condition, and have both sides sign it.
  • Recommendation Photograph the electricity, water, and gas meter readings (sayaç), with the date visible.
  • Recommendation Test taps, heating (kombi), appliances, locks, and windows, and report problems in writing straight away.
  • Legal requirement When you leave, you are not responsible for normal wear and tear. The landlord must inspect the property when you return it and tell you about any defects in writing straight away (Code of Obligations, Arts. 334–335). Your move-in photos and inventory are your evidence.

If the landlord refuses a contract or disputes a payment

  1. Pause further payments until you have a written contract or a written explanation. Recommendation
  2. Ask in writing (message or email) for the contract or receipt, and keep the reply. Written messages are evidence.
  3. Collect your evidence: bank receipts, messages, the listing (screenshots), photos, and the names and numbers of everyone involved.
  4. Send formal notices in writing. Legal requirement Notice to end a residential lease must be in writing (Code of Obligations, Art. 348). A notary can deliver a formal notice.
  5. Get advice early. Your university's international student office or legal clinic may help. If you cannot afford a lawyer, Istanbul Bar Association's Legal Aid Office provides free legal help to people without the means to pay.
  6. Mediation before court. Legal requirement For disputes arising from a lease, you must apply to a mediator (arabulucu) before you can file a lawsuit (Mediation Law No. 6325, Art. 18/B). See the Ministry of Justice Mediation Department.
  7. Suspected fraud. If you paid for a listing that turns out to be fake, report it to the police without delay (emergency number 112) and keep all evidence.

Needs confirmation Every dispute depends on its facts and documents. This guide cannot tell you whether you will get money back. Get qualified local legal advice before taking formal steps.
